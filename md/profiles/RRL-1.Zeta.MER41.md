# RRL-1.Zeta.FRD.41 (MER-41)

**RRL-1.Zeta.FRD.41 (MER-41)** is a **retroviral repeat lineage** related to the **ERV-1.Zeta.FRD.41 (PRIMA-41)** endogenous retrovirus lineage. The name **MER-41** derives from *medium reiteration frequency repeat 41*, reflecting its original identification as a repetitive sequence family. MER-41 is represented in repeat libraries by the approximately 3.9-kb **MER41-int** consensus and a diverse family of associated LTR sequences.

MER41-int is extensively degraded and cannot readily be interpreted as the remains of a conventional retroviral genome. Much of the internal sequence lacks readily detectable homology to canonical retroviral proteins and may include highly divergent, rearranged or non-retroviral sequence. However, its 3′ region retains recognisable *env*-derived sequence. Amino-acid-level comparison of this region reveals homology to **ERV-1.Zeta.FRD.41 (PRIMA-41)**, providing evidence for the phylogenetic affiliation of the retroviral component of MER-41.

MER-41 is distinguished from PRIMA-41 by its characteristic repeat architecture and associated LTRs, and is therefore classified here as a **retroviral repeat lineage (RRL)** rather than as an ERV sublineage. This designation distinguishes the recurrent MER-41 repeat family from individual truncated, rearranged or otherwise degraded loci belonging to conventional ERV lineages.

MER-41 LTRs have been classified into several repeat subfamilies, including **MER41A, MER41B, MER41C, MER41D, MER41E, MER41F and MER41G**. These designations describe sequence variation among MER-41-associated LTRs and should not themselves be interpreted as distinct retroviral lineages.

Individual MER-41 LTRs have acquired well-characterised regulatory functions during primate evolution. In particular, members of the **MER41B** family contain STAT1-binding motifs and have been co-opted as interferon-responsive regulatory elements at multiple genomic loci. MER-41-derived LTRs have consequently been studied extensively as sources of transcription-factor-binding sites and regulatory innovation in the primate genome. These activities are properties of particular MER-41-derived loci and LTR variants rather than necessarily being general properties of the repeat lineage as a whole.
