# ERV-1.Rho.Rb (HERV-Rb)

ERV-1.Rho.Rb (HERV-Rb) is a lineage of ancient clade I endogenous retroviruses that infected the ancestors of modern primates during the Eocene epoch or earlier. Although less extensively studied than some other human endogenous retrovirus lineages, HERV-Rb is notable because it gave rise to the human gene **ERVPABLB-1**, also known as **envR(b)**.

Most HERV-Rb insertions have accumulated extensive mutations since their integration and no replication-competent representatives are known. Nevertheless, comparative genomic analyses indicate that the lineage was established in primate genomes tens of millions of years ago and forms a distinct branch within the broader diversity of clade I retroviruses.

A notable feature of the lineage is the preservation of an envelope-derived gene, **ERVPABLB-1**. Like several other host genes derived from endogenous retroviral envelope proteins, envR(b) appears to have been retained by natural selection long after the original virus became extinct. The precise biological role of envR(b) remains incompletely understood, but its preservation suggests that it may provide a beneficial function to the host.

The HERV-Rb lineage illustrates an important theme in endogenous retrovirus evolution: while most viral genes decay over time, individual genes can occasionally be retained and repurposed by host genomes. Such domesticated viral genes provide evidence that ancient retroviral infections have contributed not only to genome structure, but also to the evolution of novel host functions.

Although relatively little is currently known about the biology of HERV-Rb compared to lineages such as HERV-T, HERV-R or HERV-Fb, the preservation of envR(b) highlights the continuing evolutionary legacy of this ancient retroviral lineage.
