# ERV-1.Zeta.FRD.41 (PRIMA-41)

**ERV-1.Zeta.FRD.41 (PRIMA-41)** is an ancient endogenous gammaretrovirus lineage belonging to the proposed **Zeta** genus within the **Clade I** radiation of human ERVs. It forms part of the **HERV-FRD-like** group and is represented in the human genome by a small number of highly degraded proviral loci. Comparative genomic evidence indicates that PRIMA-41 entered the genomes of ancestral simian primates tens of millions of years ago and has remained inactive since.

PRIMA-41 is closely associated with the **MER41** repeat family, which is derived largely from the lineage's LTRs and adjacent genomic regions. Consequently, PRIMA41 and MER41 have frequently been conflated in the historical literature, despite referring to different biological entities. Whereas PRIMA-41 denotes the ancestral proviral lineage, **MER41** represents a collection of derived repetitive elements and LTR subfamilies generated through the subsequent fragmentation and proliferation of these ancient insertions.

Although the PRIMA-41 proviral lineage itself has received relatively little direct study, MER41-derived repeats have become well known for their contribution to host gene regulation. Several MER41 LTR subfamilies have been co-opted as interferon-responsive regulatory elements in mammalian genomes, illustrating how fragments of an ancient retroviral lineage have been repeatedly repurposed during host evolution. Current evidence suggests that this regulatory biology is associated primarily with specific MER41-derived repeat families rather than with intact PRIMA-41 proviruses.
