# ERV-1.Iota.I (HERV-I)

**ERV-1.Iota.I (HERV-I)** is a relatively low-copy primate ERV lineage represented in the human genome by a small number of defective but recognisable proviral insertions. Orthologous HERV-I loci shared by humans, great apes and Old World monkeys show that the lineage was already present in the catarrhine germ line by the early Oligocene, approximately **30 million years ago**.

The historical name HERV-I derives from its primer binding site, which is typically complementary to an **isoleucine-specific tRNA**. Although all known human HERV-I elements are defective, several retain substantial proviral structure, allowing the lineage and its evolutionary relationships to be reconstructed.

Phylogenetic analyses place HERV-I within a deeply divergent vertebrate retroviral radiation that includes endogenous retroviruses identified in mammals, birds, reptiles and cartilaginous fishes. This radiation is distinct from the lineage containing modern gammaretroviruses, despite their deeper shared ancestry.
