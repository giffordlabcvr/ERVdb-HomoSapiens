# ERV-2.Lenti.pSIV1

**ERV-2.Lenti.pSIV1** is an endogenous lentivirus lineage identified in the grey mouse lemur (*Microcebus murinus*). Originally reported as **grey mouse lemur prosimian immunodeficiency virus (PSIVgml)**, it was the first endogenous lentivirus discovered in a primate and demonstrated that primate lentiviruses have a much deeper evolutionary history than suggested by contemporary virus sequences alone.

The discovery was particularly significant because Madagascar has been geographically isolated from mainland Africa for >150 million years. Since the ancestors of modern lemurs are thought to have colonised Madagascar approximately 50–60 million years ago, the presence of endogenous lentiviruses in Malagasy primates immediately raised questions about the antiquity of primate lentiviruses and the routes by which they spread among mammalian hosts.

Reconstruction of the pSIV1 genome revealed the characteristic organisation of a complex lentivirus, including the accessory genes *tat* and *vif* together with a putative rev gene. Building on the earlier discovery of rabbit endogenous lentivirus K (RELIK), pSIV1 extended this evidence to primates and showed that key features of lentiviral genome architecture were already established millions of years before the emergence of modern simian immunodeficiency viruses.

Phylogenetic analyses place pSIV1 within the broader 'neolentivirus' radiation that includes all known modern lentiviruses, as well as endogenous lentiviruses identified in rabbits and mustelids.
