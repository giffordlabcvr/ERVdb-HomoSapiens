# Spumavirus

**Spumavirus** is a genus within the subfamily **Spumaretrovirinae** and is assigned to **Clade III** in ERVdb. The group encompasses the foamy viruses of mammals together with related endogenous retroviruses identified in other vertebrate classes. ERVdb divides the genus into four broad, host-associated subgenera: **mammalian**, **reptilian**, **amphibian** and **coelacanth**.

This arrangement differs from current ICTV taxonomy, which assigns separate genus-level names—including **Bovispumavirus**, **Equispumavirus**, **Felispumavirus**, **Prosimiispumavirus** and **Simispumavirus**—to major lineages of mammalian foamy viruses. For practical classification across the much wider diversity revealed by ERVs, ERVdb treats these as subdivisions of the mammalian spumavirus group rather than as separate genera.

Known exogenous spumaviruses infect a diverse range of mammals, including primates, cattle, horses and cats. They typically establish persistent infections without clearly defined disease, although they can produce marked cytopathic effects in cell culture, including the characteristic foamy appearance from which their name derives.

The endogenous record shows that spumaviruses have a much deeper history and broader host distribution than their known exogenous representatives suggest. Endogenous spumaviruses have been identified in mammals, reptiles, amphibians and coelacanths, demonstrating that the group has been associated with vertebrates for hundreds of millions of years.

Spumaviruses differ from most other retroviruses in their expression of *pol*. Rather than producing Pol primarily as part of a Gag–Pol fusion, they express it independently as a Pro–Pol polyprotein. Reverse transcription also occurs to a substantial extent before virion release, so infectious particles can contain viral DNA as well as RNA.
