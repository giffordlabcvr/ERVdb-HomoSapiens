# Zetaretrovirus

*Zetaretrovirus* is an ERVdb-defined genus of exclusively endogenous retroviruses assigned to *Clade I*. It is not currently recognised as a formal taxon by the ICTV. The genus encompasses a large and diverse radiation of mammalian ERVs and is divided in ERVdb into three principal subgenera: *FRD*, *HF* and *W9P*.

The *FRD* group includes the human ERV-FRD, ERV-4, MER66 and PRIMA-41 lineages, together with a related equine zetaretrovirus. The *HF* group includes the extensive ERV-H radiation and the ERV-Fa, ERV-Fb, ERV-Fc, ERV-46 and ERV-XA lineages, as well as related elements identified in other mammals. The *W9P* group includes the human ERV-W, ERV-9, ERV-P and ERV-30 lineages. These divisions represent major phylogenetic branches within the zetaretroviral radiation rather than groupings based solely on historical nomenclature.

Zetaretroviruses are especially diverse in primate genomes, where different lineages record a succession of germ-line invasions extending across much of primate evolution. Related elements in horses, carnivores and other mammals show that the former host range of the genus was not restricted to primates. No exogenous zetaretrovirus has been identified.

Several zetaretroviral *env* genes have been co-opted during mammalian evolution. *Syncytin-1*, derived from an ERV-W *env*, and *Syncytin-2*, derived from an ERV-FRD *env*, contribute to placental development in primates. *Suppressyn*, derived from an ERV-Fb *env*, binds the amino-acid transporter ASCT2 and can inhibit infection by retroviruses that use this receptor. These examples illustrate how the zetaretroviral fossil record has contributed functional genes as well as regulatory sequences to mammalian genomes.
