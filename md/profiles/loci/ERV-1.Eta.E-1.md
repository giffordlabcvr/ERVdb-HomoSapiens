# ERV-1.Eta.E-1 (ERVE-1)

The human locus **ERV-1.Eta.E-1** (HGNC symbol **ERVE-1**) is a HERV-E-derived proviral insertion located on chromosome 17. Unlike most endogenous retroviral loci, it has been assigned an HGNC gene symbol and is annotated as a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), reflecting evidence that the locus is transcriptionally active. Transcription is thought to be initiated from the proviral long terminal repeat (LTR), illustrating how endogenous retroviral regulatory sequences can become integrated into host gene expression networks.

Expression of ERVE-1 has been detected in several normal tissues, including the pancreas, thyroid and placenta. The LTR promoter exhibits tissue-specific DNA methylation patterns, being relatively hypomethylated in placental cells but more heavily methylated in blood and other somatic tissues. These observations suggest that epigenetic regulation plays an important role in controlling transcription from the locus.

Although ERV-1.Eta.E-1 retains no known protein-coding capacity, its RNA transcript and LTR promoter have attracted interest as potential regulators of host gene expression. As with many transcribed endogenous retroviral loci, however, its biological function remains uncertain.

Altered expression of ERVE-1 has been reported in autoimmune diseases including systemic lupus erythematosus and multiple sclerosis. Current evidence is limited to observational studies, and it remains unclear whether these changes contribute to disease pathogenesis or simply reflect broader alterations in cellular transcriptional and epigenetic states.
