# ERV-1.Gamma.Eta.R-1 (ERV3)

**ERV-1.Gamma.Eta.R-1** is a human endogenous retrovirus locus belonging to the **ERV-1.Gamma.R** lineage. It corresponds to the HGNC gene **ERV3-1** and was historically known as **ERV3**, **HERV-R**, **ERV-R**, or **envR**.

The human copy of ERV-1.Gamma.Eta.R-1 is located on chromosome 7 and is shared across Catarrhine primates, indicating an ancient integration event approximately 30--40 million years ago. The locus has retained an intact envelope (**env**) open reading frame and exhibits broad transcriptional activity in normal tissues.

Another notable feature of ERV-1.Gamma.Eta.R-1 is its close association with the neighbouring zinc-finger gene **ZNF117**. Read-through transcription from the ERV3 promoter can generate transcripts containing **ZNF117** sequences, effectively allowing the endogenous retrovirus to function as an alternative promoter for a host gene. This relationship has attracted interest because it illustrates one way in which endogenous retroviruses can become integrated into host gene regulatory networks.

ERV3-1 expression has been reported in a wide variety of normal tissues, particularly during placental development and cellular differentiation. The locus has also been investigated in autoimmune diseases and several forms of cancer, although its precise biological role remains uncertain. While some studies have suggested physiological functions for the ERV3-1 envelope protein, others have proposed that its expression may simply reflect broader changes in cellular regulatory states.
