# ERV-1.Gamma.R-3 (ERV3)

The human copy of locus ERV-1.Gamma.R-3 (ERV3) is located on chromosome 7. This insertion is shared across Catarrhine primates, indicating an ancient integration event approximately 30-40 million years ago. Unlike most HERV loci, which are highly fragmented and largely unstudied, ERV3 has retained an intact envelope (env) open reading frame and exhibits broad transcriptional activity in normal tissues.

A distinctive feature of ERV3 is its close association with the neighbouring zinc-finger gene **ZNF117**. Read-through transcription from the ERV3 promoter can generate transcripts containing ZNF117 sequences, effectively allowing the endogenous retrovirus to function as an alternative promoter for a host gene. This relationship has attracted considerable interest because it illustrates one way in which endogenous retroviruses can become integrated into host gene regulatory networks.

ERV3 expression has been reported in a wide variety of normal tissues, particularly during placental development and cellular differentiation. The locus has also been investigated in autoimmune diseases and several forms of cancer, although its precise biological role remains uncertain. While some studies have suggested physiological functions for the ERV3 envelope protein, others have proposed that ERV3 expression may simply reflect broader changes in cellular regulatory states.
