# ERV-1.Zeta.W9.W-1 (Syncytin-1 / ERVW-1)

The human locus **ERV-1.Zeta.W9.W-1** (_ERVW-1_), commonly known as **Syncytin-1**, is a domesticated _env_ gene derived from the HERV-W lineage. Orthologous insertions shared by hominoids indicate that the ancestral HERV-W provirus entered the germ line before the divergence of modern apes, after which its envelope gene was maintained by purifying selection while the remainder of the provirus accumulated disabling mutations.

Syncytin-1 is expressed in placental trophoblast cells, where it mediates the fusion of cytotrophoblasts to form the syncytiotrophoblast. The protein retains the membrane fusion activity of a retroviral envelope glycoprotein and is essential for normal placental development.

The discovery of Syncytin-1 provided one of the first clear demonstrations that endogenous retroviral genes can be co-opted to perform essential host functions. Together with **Syncytin-2** and other independently acquired syncytins, it illustrates the repeated recruitment of retroviral _env_ genes during the evolution of placental mammals.
