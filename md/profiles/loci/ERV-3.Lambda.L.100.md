# ERV-3.Lambda.L.100

**ERV-3.Lambda.L.100** is a highly degraded ERV-L-derived proviral insertion located on human chromosome 17. Orthologous copies were identified across all four major placental mammal superorders, demonstrating that the insertion predates their divergence and has a minimum age of approximately **104--110 million years**.

The locus contains no intact retroviral open reading frames and has been extensively disrupted by mutation, deletion and the insertion of other repetitive elements. Nevertheless, conserved flanking sequences, shared internal insertions and broadly host-congruent phylogenetic relationships establish that the sequences found in different mammals derive from the same ancestral germline integration.

ERV-3.Lambda.L.100 therefore provides direct evidence that ERV-L viruses were infecting the ancestors of placental mammals more than 100 million years ago. Estimates based on divergence between its LTRs suggest that the original integration may have occurred considerably earlier, perhaps around 128--140 million years ago, although these estimates are less secure than the minimum age established by orthology.
