# ERV-1.Zeta.MER41 (MER-41)

**ERV-1.Zeta.MER41 (MER-41)** is an ancient, highly degraded endogenous retrovirus sublineage within the Zeta clade. The name **MER-41** derives from *medium reiteration frequency repeat 41*, reflecting its original identification as a repetitive sequence family rather than through characterization of a complete endogenous retroviral genome. MER-41 is represented in repeat libraries by the approximately 3.9-kb **MER41-int** internal consensus and a diverse family of associated LTR sequences.

MER41-int is extensively degraded and retains little readily recognisable retroviral coding structure. However, its 3′ region contains residual **env**-derived sequence. Amino-acid-level comparison of this region reveals homology to members of the Zeta clade, and to the **PRIMA-41** lineage in particular, providing sequence evidence for the phylogenetic placement of MER-41 despite the extensive degeneration of its internal sequence.

MER-41 LTRs are abundant in the human genome and have been classified into several repeat subfamilies, including **MER41A, MER41B, MER41C, MER41D, MER41E, MER41F and MER41G**. These LTR classifications describe sequence variation among MER-41-associated repeats and should not necessarily be interpreted as distinct endogenous retroviral sublineages. Historical associations between the LTR and internal-element nomenclature are also complex; notably, **MER41C has been associated with the PRIMA41 internal element**.

Individual MER-41 LTRs have acquired well-characterised regulatory functions during primate evolution. In particular, members of the MER41B family contain STAT1-binding motifs and have been co-opted as interferon-responsive regulatory elements at several genomic loci. MER-41-derived LTRs have consequently been investigated extensively as sources of transcription-factor-binding sites and regulatory innovation in the primate genome. Such activities are properties of particular MER-41-derived loci and LTR variants rather than necessarily being general properties of the sublineage as a whole.
