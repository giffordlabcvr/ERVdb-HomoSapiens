# ERV-2.Beta.Mus.D (MusD)

**ERV-2.Beta.Mus.D (MusD)** is an endogenous betaretrovirus lineage found in mice. Although derived from an ancestral infectious retrovirus, MusD has lost its *env* gene and now propagates exclusively by intracellular retrotransposition. Active MusD elements continue to generate new insertions in laboratory mouse genomes and provide the proteins required for mobilisation of the non-autonomous **ETn** retrotransposon family.

MusD retains the characteristic *gag*, *pro* and *pol* genes of a betaretrovirus and is part of the 'type D' subclade within the Betaretrovirus genus, including **Mason–Pfizer monkey virus (MPMV)**. Electron microscopy showed that MusD assembles intracellular virus-like particles that mature within the cytoplasm but are not released from the cell.

Experimental studies identified the molecular basis of this intracellular lifestyle. Unlike infectious betaretroviruses, MusD lacks a functional membrane-targeting signal in the Gag protein. Restoration of this signal redirected particle assembly to the plasma membrane and, when combined with a heterologous envelope protein, allowed production of infectious virus particles. These experiments demonstrated that relatively small genetic changes can drive the evolutionary transition between an infectious retrovirus and an intracellular retrotransposon.
