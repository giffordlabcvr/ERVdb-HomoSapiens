# ERV-1.Gamma.TR.T (HERV-T)

**ERV-1.Gamma.TR.T**, also known as *human endogenous retrovirus type T (HERV-T)*, is a lineage of mammalian gammaretroviruses that groups within the diversity of extant exogenous gammaretroviruses. Despite this, the exogenous progenitor of HERV-T appears to be extinct. Orthologous insertions indicate that HERV-T had entered the catarrhine germ line by the Late Oligocene, approximately **25 million years ago**.

Reconstruction of the ancestral HERV-T envelope (Env) protein showed that HERV-T used the cellular transporter **MCT1 (*SLC16A1*)** as its entry receptor. Remarkably, one HERV-T-derived *env* gene retained an intact open reading frame after the lineage had become extinct. Experimental studies demonstrated that the encoded envelope protein could remove MCT1 from the cell surface, thereby preventing infection by viruses using the same receptor (PMID: 28397686).

These findings led to the proposal that a HERV-T-derived envelope protein was co-opted as an antiviral restriction factor during primate evolution. If correct, this would represent a striking example of an endogenous retroviral gene protecting its host against exogenous viruses descended from the lineage from which it originated.
