# ERV-1-Gamma.Mega.2 (CfERV)

**ERV-1-Gamma.Mega.2**, also known as *canis familiaris endogenous retrovirus (CfERV)*, is the principal endogenous gammaretrovirus lineage found in domestic dogs and other members of the dog family (Canidae). Orthologous insertions shared among canids indicate that the lineage entered the genome of a common ancestor before the diversification of modern canid species (PMID: 23021955).

Although the canine genome contains relatively few endogenous retroviruses compared with many other mammals, dozens of CfERV loci have been identified. Nearly all are highly degraded, and no replication-competent CfERV virus is known. Nevertheless, some loci remain transcriptionally active in normal tissues and cultured cells.

The characterisation of CfERV helped clarify the endogenous retroviral content of the canine genome. Earlier reports of retrovirus-like particles in canine tumours had prompted speculation that dogs might harbour infectious gammaretroviruses analogous to feline leukemia virus. Instead, CfERV was shown to comprise an ancient lineage of defective endogenous retroviruses rather than an active infectious virus.
