# ERV-1.Gamma.E (HERV-E)

**ERV-1.Gamma.E (HERV-E)** is an ancient gammaretrovirus lineage that infected primate ancestors from at least the Eocene, with germline integrations continuing into the Miocene. Molecular dating of individual insertions suggests that HERV-E entered the primate germ line repeatedly between at least **50 million** and approximately **10 million years ago**, with many loci predating the divergence of Old World monkeys and apes. Today, most HERV-E loci survive only as fragmented proviruses or solo long terminal repeats (LTRs), and no infectious HERV-E virus is known. Nevertheless, numerous HERV-E-derived LTRs have been co-opted as promoters and enhancers that influence the expression of neighbouring host genes (PMID: 25785516).

Several HERV-E loci have been studied in detail because they contribute to tissue-specific gene regulation or show aberrant activation in disease. HERV-E-derived regulatory elements have been implicated in placental development, immune regulation and several cancers, while expression of specific HERV-E loci has been associated with autoimmune disease and renal cell carcinoma (PMID: 25785516).
