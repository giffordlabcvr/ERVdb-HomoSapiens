# ERV-3-Lambda.L.2 (MuERV-L)

**ERV-3-Lambda.L.2**, also known as *murine endogenous retrovirus type L (MuERV-L)* is the murine representative of the ancient ERV-L lineage, a group of endogenous retroviruses found throughout placental mammals. Unlike the fragmented ERV-L copies found in many species, the mouse genome contains numerous relatively intact MuERV-L proviruses, reflecting at least two major amplification events after the divergence of mice and rats.

MuERV-L is notable as the evolutionary source of *Fv1*, the murine restriction factor that protects mice against murine leukemia virus infection. The discovery that *Fv1* evolved from a MuERV-L *gag* gene provided one of the earliest demonstrations that endogenous retroviral genes can be co-opted for antiviral defence.

MuERV-L is also distinguished by its highly specific expression during embryogenesis. Transcription peaks at the two-cell stage following zygotic genome activation, and expression of intact MuERV-L proviruses produces the characteristic *epsilon particles* observed in early mouse embryos.
