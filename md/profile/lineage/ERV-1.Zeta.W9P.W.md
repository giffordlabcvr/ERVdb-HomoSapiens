# ERV-1.Zeta.W9.W (HERV-W)

**ERV-1.Zeta.W9.W (HERV-W)** is an ancient endogenous retrovirus lineage best known as the source of **Syncytin-1**(*ERVW-1*), an envelope-derived protein required for normal placental development in humans and other hominoids.

Orthologous **Syncytin-1** loci indicate that the HERV-W *env* gene was domesticated during the Miocene, following the insertion of the ancestral HERV-W provirus in the common ancestor of hominoids. Unlike most HERV-W loci, which have accumulated extensive mutations, the Syncytin-1 *env* gene has been maintained by purifying selection. It retains the membrane fusion activity of a retroviral envelope protein and is expressed in the syncytiotrophoblast, where it mediates trophoblast fusion during placental development.

No replication-competent HERV-W viruses are known, although numerous degraded HERV-W loci remain distributed throughout the human genome.
