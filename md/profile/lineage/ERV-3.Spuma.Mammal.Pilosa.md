# ERV-3.Spuma.Mammal.Pilosa (SloEFV)

**ERV-3.Spuma.Mammal.Pilosa** is an endogenous foamy virus lineage found in sloths (Pilosa). Originally reported as sloth endogenous foamy virus (SloEFV), it was the first endogenous foamy virus to be identified and demonstrates that foamy viruses have infected mammals since at least the early Cenozoic, with orthologous insertions indicating that the lineage entered the genome of an ancestral sloth between the divergence of sloths from anteaters (~55 million years ago) and the diversification of living sloths (~21 million years ago) (PMID: 19762636).

Phylogenetic analyses showed that SloEFV groups robustly with contemporary foamy viruses and retains the characteristic genomic organisation of the genus, including homologues of the accessory genes **tas** and **bet**. Reconstruction of a consensus SloEFV genome demonstrated that the overall genome structure of foamy viruses has remained remarkably conserved despite tens of millions of years of evolution (PMID: 19762636).

The discovery of SloEFV provided the first direct evidence that foamy viruses have co-diverged with their mammalian hosts over deep evolutionary timescales. Combined with the geographic isolation of xenarthrans in South America, the phylogenetic congruence between foamy viruses and their hosts supports a long history of virus-host coevolution extending back to the early diversification of placental mammals.
