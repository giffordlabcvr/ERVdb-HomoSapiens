# ERV-2.Beta.Archeo.9 (ERV-Hako)

**ERV-2.Beta.Archeo.9**, also known as *ERV-Hako*, is a distinct lineage of ancient human endogenous retroviruses within the betaretroviral radiation (genus *Betaretrovirus*). Phylogenetic analysis of gag--pol sequences places this lineage in the vicinity of HERV-K-HML5, but supports its recognition as a separate evolutionary group.

The lineage appears to correspond to a group designated *HML9* by Katzourakis and Tristem in their contribution to the edited volume *Retroviruses and Primate Genome* (Sverdlov, 2005). This designation should not be confused with the independently defined HML9 group of the Blomberg/RepBase classification, which corresponds to HERVK14C. The use of the same designation for two phylogenetically distinct ERV groups represents a historical nomenclatural ambiguity.

More recently, Frith (2025) independently characterised the lineage as *ERV-Hako*, identifying proviral remnants and associated LTRs in primate genomes. Notably, some ERV-Hako elements contain sequences derived from host genes, including *SUSD6* and *SPHKAP*. Comparative genomic evidence indicates that ERV-Hako was present in ancestral primates and that a variant containing *SUSD6* sequence was transferred between catarrhine and platyrrhine lineages (PMID: 40083010).

Recognition of ERV-2.Beta.Archeo.9 as a distinct lineage reconciles these historical and recent observations within a phylogenetically grounded classification of human endogenous retroviruses.
