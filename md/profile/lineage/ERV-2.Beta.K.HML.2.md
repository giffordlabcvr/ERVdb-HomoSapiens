# ERV-2.Beta.K.HML.2 (HERV-K-HML2)

**ERV-2.Beta.K.HML.2**, also known as *human endogenous retrovirus K, human mouse-like 2 (HERV-K-HML2)* is the youngest and best-preserved lineage within the *K.HML* sublineage of betaretroviruses (genus *Betaretrovirus*). Unlike the more ancient HML lineages, HML2 remained active throughout much of hominoid evolution, generating germline integrations from the early Miocene until the Late Pleistocene (approximately 30 million to <1 million years ago). The human reference genome contains around 90 proviral loci together with hundreds of solo LTRs, and numerous additional insertions are polymorphic within modern human populations. Several integrations are unique to humans, making HML2 the only HERV lineage known to have continued expanding after the divergence of humans and chimpanzees.

HML2 is distinguished by the exceptional preservation of its proviruses. Many loci retain long uninterrupted open reading frames, and several preserve complete or nearly complete viral genes, including the lineage-specific accessory proteins Rec and Np9 (PMID: 15063128, PMID: 26103464). However, there is no evidence that any human insertion is capable of producing an infectious virus.

The close relationship among HML2 proviruses has enabled the reconstruction of infectious ancestral viruses from consensus sequences, including the independently derived *Phoenix* and *HERV-KCON* genomes (PMID: 17257061, PMID: 17077319). These studies demonstrated that inferred ancestral HML2 viruses encoded functional structural proteins, enzymes and envelope glycoproteins capable of supporting a complete retroviral replication cycle in cultured cells, providing the first experimental reconstruction of an extinct human endogenous retrovirus.

HML2 is also the most intensively studied HERV lineage with respect to transcriptional activity. Genome-wide analyses have shown that dozens of individual HML2 proviruses are expressed in normal human tissues, with distinct tissue-specific expression patterns and evidence for both LTR-driven and host-driven transcription. Expression of HML2 loci is also frequently observed in embryonic tissues, cancers and other pathological conditions, although the functional significance of most loci remains uncertain. Together with its recent evolutionary origin and unusually intact proviral repertoire, this persistent transcriptional activity has established HML2 as the principal experimental model for understanding the biology and evolution of human endogenous retroviruses (PMID: 36256614).
