# ERV-3.Spuma.Sphenodon (ERV-Spuma-Spu)

**ERV-3.Spuma.Sphenodon** is an endogenous foamy virus lineage found in the tuatara (*Sphenodon punctatus*). Originally reported as ERV-Spuma-Spu, it was the first endogenous foamy virus identified in a reptile. Divergence between the paired long terminal repeats (LTRs) of five proviral copies yielded integration age estimates of approximately 1.3--35.5 million years, based on a neutral substitution rate estimated for squamates (PMID: 30838130).

Consensus genome reconstructions revealed the characteristic foamy virus *gag-pol-env* organisation. A subsequent reconstruction recovered longer LTRs and suggested that the putative accessory open reading frame described in the original study arose from a single-base difference between the two consensus sequences (PMIDs: 30838130, 31336856).

The discovery extended the known host range of foamy viruses to reptiles and raised the possibility that the tuatara and mammalian foamy virus lineages diverged alongside their hosts more than 320 million years ago. Subsequent analyses recovered different relationships for Pol and Env. Tuatara Pol grouped with the coelacanth endogenous foamy virus, whereas tuatara Env grouped more closely with mammalian foamy viruses. This discordance leaves the proposed codivergence unresolved and suggests a more complex evolutionary history for reptilian foamy viruses (PMIDs: 30838130, 31336856).
