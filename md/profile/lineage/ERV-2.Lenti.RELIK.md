# ERV-2.Lenti.RELIK (RELIK)

**ERV-2.Lenti.RELIK (RELIK; Rabbit Endogenous Lentivirus type K)** is an endogenous lentivirus lineage identified in rabbits and hares. It was the first endogenous lentivirus to be discovered, demonstrating that lentiviruses have infected mammals since at least the Miocene.

Orthologous RELIK insertions shared by rabbits and hares indicate that the lineage entered the lagomorph germ line before their divergence approximately **12 million years ago**, providing the first robust minimum age calibration for the lentivirus genus.

Although all known RELIK loci are degraded, reconstruction of the ancestral virus revealed the characteristic genome organisation of a complex lentivirus, including the accessory genes *tat*, *rev* and *vif*. Experimental reconstruction of ancestral RELIK proteins subsequently enabled direct investigation of the biology of an extinct lentivirus.

Phylogenetically, RELIK belongs within the lineage containing modern lentiviruses and demonstrated that the defining features of the genus had already evolved by the Miocene. Its discovery transformed understanding of lentivirus evolution and established ERVs as an important source of evidence for reconstructing the deep history of the Lentivirus genus.
