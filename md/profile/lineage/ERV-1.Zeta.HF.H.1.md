# ERV-1.Zeta.HF.H.1 (HERV-H)

**ERV-1.Zeta.HF.H.1 (HERV-H)** is a primate ERV lineage whose ancestors underwent a major expansion during primate evolution, leaving hundreds of HERV-H-derived elements in the human genome. Much of this material persists as solitary LTRs or structurally degraded proviruses, making HERV-H a prominent component of the human ERV repertoire.

Although all known HERV-H proviruses are defective, many retain transcriptionally active long terminal repeats (LTRs). HERV-H is particularly notable for its activity in pluripotent stem cells, where HERV-H-derived LTRs function as promoters and enhancers and HERV-H transcripts are among the most abundant retroviral RNAs expressed in human embryonic stem cells. These regulatory sequences have become integrated into transcriptional networks associated with pluripotency and early embryonic development.
