# Clade I

*Clade I* is one of three major retroviral groups recognised in ERVdb. It comprises the formally recognised genera *Epsilonretrovirus* and *Gammaretrovirus*, together with the ERVdb-defined *Iotaretrovirus*, *Rhoretrovirus*, *Thetaretrovirus*, *Xiretrovirus* and *Zetaretrovirus* groups. Clade I represents a deep phylogenetic grouping rather than a formal ICTV taxon.

Clade I retroviruses have been identified in a broad range of vertebrate hosts. Known exogenous members include gammaretroviruses of mammals and birds and epsilonretroviruses of fish, while the endogenous record reveals a much wider and more ancient diversity. Clade I ERVs occur throughout vertebrate genomes and include numerous lineages for which no closely related exogenous viruses are currently known.

A characteristic feature of Clade I is the use of translational readthrough in the expression of *pol*. In well-characterised members, suppression of the termination codon separating *gag* and *pol* permits production of a Gag–Pol polyprotein. This contrasts with the programmed ribosomal frameshifting characteristic of Clade II.
